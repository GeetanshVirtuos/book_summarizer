## The “New You” Business: Transforming Industries Through Customer Outcomes

The article "The New You" Business argues that businesses across industries (fitness, healthcare, 
education, and more) are missing a crucial opportunity. Instead of solely focusing on improving the 
quality, convenience, and experience of what they sell – equipment, procedures, courses – organizations 
should prioritize helping customers achieve *desired outcomes* and fundamentally transform their lives. 
The core concept revolves around shifting from providing goods and services to facilitating profound, 
lasting change for individuals.

**The Limitations of Traditional Approaches**

Current business models often fail because they overlook the underlying *reason* people seek their 
offerings. Fitness centers are about getting in shape, not just using equipment. Healthcare is about 
wellness, not just prescriptions. Education is about acquiring skills, not just accumulating knowledge. 
The problem isn’s the offerings themselves, but rather the fragmented nature of achieving those larger 
goals. Customers are left to cobble together disparate resources (a trainer, a diet plan, a support 
network) to achieve their desired transformations.

**Introducing the Transformation Business**

The "New You" business model centers on partnering with customers to help them realize these 
aspirations. This requires a fundamental shift in perspective, embracing five key characteristics:

1.  **Selling a Distinct Economic Offering:** Moving beyond commodities, goods, and services, a 
transformation business creates a distinct economic offering – a pathway to profound personal change.
2.  **Focusing on Customer Success:**  Success is defined by the customer's desired outcome, becoming 
the North Star guiding all business decisions.
3.  **Having a Solutions Mindset:**  Instead of providing isolated pieces, transformation businesses 
offer complete solutions, partnering with others when necessary, to ensure customers have everything 
they need.
4.  **Integrating Solutions:** Combining services, support, and resources to streamline the entire 
customer journey.
5.  **Charging for Outcomes:** Aligning fees with actual results, creating a shared interest in 
customer success.



**Understanding Transformations: A Three-Step Process**

To design effective transformation offerings, businesses must:

1.  **Ascertain the “Jobs to be Done”:** Identify the underlying functions, emotions, social desires, 
and (most importantly) *aspirational* goals customers seek. This goes beyond surface-level requests to 
uncover the deeper motivations driving behavior. Techniques like asking "5 whys" help uncover these 
hidden aspirations.
2.  **Define Success Along the Way:** Map the entire customer journey and define what success looks 
like at each stage. It's not enough to just define the end goal; businesses need to understand the 
milestones and challenges along the way.
3.  **Identify Barriers:**  Determine the obstacles preventing customers from achieving their desired 
outcomes. These barriers exist in resources, readiness, and context.



**Illustrative Case Studies**

*   **Profile by Sanford:** This weight-loss program exemplifies the transformation business model. 
Instead of simply providing a diet plan, Profile provides a personalized, keto-based regimen with 
weekly coaching, meal planning support, and behavior change guidance. The pledge system, where 
customers commit to the *process* rather than just a weight-loss target, is crucial.
*   **Noom:** This program emphasizes behavior change through daily lessons and coaching, guiding 
individuals towards sustainable lifestyle transformations.
*   **Georgia State University:** By leveraging predictive analytics and personalized support, Georgia 
State significantly improved graduation rates, demonstrating the power of addressing student needs and 
removing barriers.
*   **Resolve Solutions Incorporated (RSI):** This organization supports underprivileged youths, 
guiding them through education, career development, and providing essential mentorship.

**Key Differentiators of the Transformation Business**

The article highlights several key advantages of the transformation business model:

*   **Harder to Imitate:** Unlike traditional offerings, transformations involve deeply integrated 
solutions and ongoing customer partnerships, making them difficult to replicate.
*   **Shared Responsibility:** The transformation business model emphasizes the customer's active 
participation and commitment, creating a shared responsibility for success.
*   **Focus on Long-Term Value:** By focusing on outcomes, businesses can build long-term customer 
relationships and create lasting value.




**Conclusion**

The “New You” Business model represents a significant shift in how businesses operate. By shifting 
focus from merely selling goods or services to facilitating customer transformation, businesses can 
unlock new opportunities for growth, innovation, and – most importantly – make a meaningful difference 
in people’s lives. The article encourages businesses across all industries to embrace this approach, 
focusing on outcomes and partnering with customers to help them achieve their full potential.
