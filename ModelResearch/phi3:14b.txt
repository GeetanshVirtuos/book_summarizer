Summary: "The 'New You' Business" by Lance A. Bettencourt, B. Joseph Pine II, James H. Gilmore and 
David W. Norton - January–February 2022 Edition of Harvard Business Review

Introduction: The article explores the concept of transformation in various industries like fitness 
centers, medical providers, colleges, etc., focusing on helping customers achieve a "new you" rather 
than just selling products or services. It emphasizes that while quality, convenience, and experience 
are important, they are means to an end - customer success.

Main Points:
1. Transformation Business: The article discusses the transformation business as a progression beyond 
experiences, involving partnerships with customers for significant life changes. This includes 
addressing functional, emotional, social, and aspirational jobs in order to achieve desired results.
2. Design Solutions: Companies must offer complete solutions that cover all aspects of what consumers 
need for success; otherwise, individuals will struggle to accomplish change on their own. The article 
discusses Profile by Sanford as an example of a company providing comprehensive weight loss 
transformation services.
3. Defining Successful Transformation: To design effective transformation offerings, companies must 
understand customers' jobs to be done (functional, emotional, social, and aspirational), define success 
along the way, and identify barriers that hinder achievement of goals. The article suggests using 
methods like interviews, observations, ethnography, etc., for this purpose.
4. Case Study - TrueConnect: The financial benefit provider helps people with poor credit scores obtain 
manageable loans through their employers. It also offers free counseling on topics such as budget 
creation and debt reduction to guide customers towards financial wellness.
5. Designing the Offering: To create a successful transformation offering, companies can integrate 
solutions, engage each customer as an essential partner, provide customized support, cover all 
jobs-to-be-done, charge for outcomes, etc. The article provides examples of Noom (weight loss), 
Navigate (student success at Georgia State University), and Resolve Solutions Incorporated (youth 
career aspirations) to illustrate these approaches.
6. Transformation Business Models: These models are much harder to imitate than traditional ones but 
promise significant rewards, as they focus on transformational outcomes rather than just selling goods 
or services. Competing in the transformation business requires companies to work closely with customers 
and align fees with what customers value - their desired transformations.
7. Conclusion: The "New You" Business promises handsome rewards not only financially but also through 
making a profound difference in customers' lives, generating knowledge of having truly made an impact 
on them.
